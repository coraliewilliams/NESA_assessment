## Step 0 — data exploration findings

**Shape & structure**
- marks.csv: 1000 students × 49 paper columns (+ `student_id`).
- 20 subjects, 49 papers total (1–4 papers each). Weights within every subject sum exactly to 100 — confirmed for all 20 subjects, no exceptions.
- Papers with only **one** paper (no same-subject partner to borrow from): `MAT-100`, `BIO-100`, `CHE-100`, `GEO-100`, `MED-100`.

**Missingness**
- 49,000 total cells: 38,034 blank (77.6%, "not registered"), 181 `M` (0.37%), 10,785 numeric (22%).
- Confirmed **blank and `M` never overlap** — clean categorical distinction, no parsing ambiguity.
- `M` per column: 1–7, roughly uniform across columns (no column is a special case).
- `M` per row: 945 students have 0 `M`s; 55 students have ≥1 (up to 9 in one extreme case). Of the 181 `M` cells, **14 sit in single-paper subjects** (`MAT`, `BIO`, `CHE`, `GEO`, `MED`) where no same-subject sibling paper exists to borrow from — these need a different fallback.

**Distributions (proportion-of-max scale)**
- Per-paper mean proportion ranges ~0.58–0.78, SD ~0.10–0.13. No values pile up at 0 or 1 (min/max well inside bounds), roughly symmetric — no strong skew, but bounded [0,1] data with mean-dependent variance (binomial-like) still justifies a **logit transform** for modelling, mainly to guarantee predictions can't leave [0,1] and to stabilize variance rather than to fix skew.

**Correlation structure — this is the key driver of method choice**
- **Within-subject** (a student's papers in the same subject): strong and consistent, r ≈ 0.51–0.68 across every multi-paper subject. This is the dominant, reliable signal.
- **Cross-subject** (a student's average performance in one subject vs. another): weak and noisy, roughly −0.47 to 0.38, no stable sign or consistent global "ability" factor — likely a mix of true weak association and small pairwise sample sizes.
- **Implication**: same-subject information should carry most of the imputation weight; a global model that pools across *all* subjects (e.g. a single student ability effect) has much less signal to offer here, and I should test that empirically rather than assume it helps.

**Missingness mechanism**: stated cause is illness/misadventure — plausibly **MCAR** (chance events unrelated to ability) but with an **MNAR** risk if illness also suppressed the student's marks in nearby papers they *did* sit (then our same-subject predictor would be biased). Illness is very unlikely to be "MAR-only" in the sense of some other papers' scores being enough to reconstruct the missing one, so I'll flag this uncertainty rather than assume MAR cleanly holds.
